Michael is the second and youngest child of Noelle and the Reverend Edward Scott, vicar of Drombog Parish Church, Ballyparable Road. Situated in the suburban countryside of South West Belfast. It was here amid the well-tended garden of the “Green Manse” rectory, that Michael passed an idyllic childhood, lost in boyish rapture gazing out from his “Bird’s Nest”, as the children’s treehouse was jocularly referred to by their father. The blunt matter-of-factness of Ulster society did not start to take its toll upon this fresh new spirit until the process of formal education began. The expansive mood of 60’s mass culture clashed sourly with the boredom of grammar-school-days. Encouraged by his mother, Michael fed his starved imagination with the images of Art. Noelle Scott in her youth had entertained dreams of becoming a professional painter. A dream she had too easily let herself be discouraged from, thus her hopes for her son. With the eruption of The Troubles in 196?, young Michael, on the threshold of a wild adolescence, turned in on himself and dreamt of a Gauguinesque escape. His elder sister, Janet, by contrast forged ahead at school and, after graduating the Oldcourt Institute in London, embarked upon a career as an art journalist.
