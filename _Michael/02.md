&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Among some trees the fruit is found to start soft and tender. Gradually as its season progresses becoming hard and dry.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;In others the fruit likewise starting soft and tender gradually turns rotten and decays.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Both fruits contain seed. The first within its husk, the second within its flesh.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The husk being unpalatable remains of little interest to Fruit-seekers. Undisturbed it falls directly to the ground below. In time becoming buried there. Thus is the seed in the husk planted.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The desire of the Fruit-seekers for flesh in all its sweet succulence results on the other hand, in it being invariably consumed and digested. The seed spilled in time on distant soil. Thus the seed in the flesh is planted.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A certain irony is to be noted in considering how seed in both cases is protected, that when deliciousness of the flesh is present the husk it hides within is in truth often harder than the husk which flesh does not enclose.
