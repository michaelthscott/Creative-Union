As the bombs were falling on Guernica and screams rent the Spanish air, many miles to the north amid the green hills of County Down, the Reverend Edward Scott proposed marriage to his gentle sweetheart, Noelle Ireland Rippleham. She accepted.

Drumbog Parish Church nestles mid trees and graves. Ballyparable Crossroads, the heart of the suburban countryside south of Belfast. Here, in the love-tended garden of "Greenmanse", Michael passed an idyllic childhood. Lost in enraptured speculation, gazing out from his "Bedroom", as his treehouse had jocularly been christened by his father.

Michael was the youngest of two children. His sister, Janet, being some five years his senior, he was indulged in his solitary habits.

The blunt matter-of-factness of Ulster society did not start to take its toll upon this fresh new spirit until the process of formal education began. The expansive mood of 60's mass culture clashed sourly with the boredom of grammar-school-days. Encouraged by his mother Michael fed his now starving imagination with the images of Art.

Mrs Scott had in her youth entertained dreams of becoming a professional painter. A dream she had let herself be discouraged from, thus her hopes for her son.

With the eruption of The Troubles in 196? young Michael, on the threshold of a wild adolescence, turned yet further in on himself and dreamt Gauguinesque escape. Janet, by contrast forged ahead at school and after graduating from The Oldcourt Institute in London, embarked upon a career as an art journalist.

Death of his mother in 198?. Michael goes messianic. The reclusive pastoral dreamer unexpectedly arrives in London; telling his sister, "You've got to help me, I'm a genius."

Studio was found, materials and equipment purchased. It is to her credit that at this time Janet made considerable sacrifices and went out of her way to help her brother. Her friends and acquaintances of that time often judged it to be her folly, and true she did often think of it that way, referring to her burden as that of Theo's, the brother of Van Gogh.

Shock of Big City. Indifference by the multitude.

Years pass. One night, returning across the Hungerford Bridge Janet found her brother shedding drunken tears into the black Thames below. "My vision's marginalisin' me outa eggzistance!"
Weeks later Michael suffered a complete nervous collapse.

Chance would have it that at the time this happened Janet was hot on the scent of an Art Scoop. Toying with the idea of a biography of Joanna Bonger, wife of Theo Van Gogh, Janet had come across a series of veiled remarks in her correspondence with a mysterious Professor T who had in his possession "certain drawings" for which he thought the Dutch Government would "wish to be the highest bidder." Holmesian analysis had led her to "Vincent House" a special care sanatorium. run by a Prof. Tersteeg, a libertarian Reichian therapist.

Trojan-horselike Michael was offered to Prof. Tersteeg for treatment.

Events overtook Janet at this point. A whirlwind marriage just as quickly followed by disaster. This story does not belong here suffice to say that for almost two years her father believed that she was lost, presumed dead.

Reverend Scott sought now that his poor demented son be returned to Northern Ireland and installed in the Municipal Asylum. Tersteeg inexplicably was opposed to this. Enraged, Reverend Scott came to London.

This is where I come in.

Knowing of my days in America as a "Bible salesman", my old chesspal, none other than Prof Tersteeg, acquainted me with the situation, asking me to arbitrate in the matter. He said it would kill the boy to go back to Belfast.

Cutting a long story short I knew that Michael was to be my artist. Reverend Scott and I got on like a house on fire. The matter was solved. Michael would remain.
