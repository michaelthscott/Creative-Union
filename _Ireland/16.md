Patrick O'Conner
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;to Lord Voluminus Tome

Sire,
There has been this laft Year a curious Perfonage at loofe about this Land of Irelande; And as it may perhaps amufe Your Lordship to perufe some details of this Character, I fhall endeavour to put down here all that I have been able to afertain.\
On the twelth day of July there appeared amongft the crowded Market-place of Ennifkillin, a large and forceful Man leading a mule on which appeared to be fat a fmall Child all wrapt in tartan blanket. No part of this Child was to be feen.\
As he forced his way to the Centre of the crowded Square, the Man exorted the People to heed his words: "The third Age has begun! I bring thee Light! We have entered the Age of the holy Fpirit!"\
Much banter and derifion was the reply of the Populace. Yet the Man, being large of phyfique and charifmatic in manner, held to his courfe and arrived at a fmall Platform which had been erected in the Fquare for fome Municipal purpofe. With tender delicafy he remove the Child from the mule and mounted with him unto the Platform.\
With eyes of fire he commanded filence. Never was a man more fitted for the pulpit than this man. His words filled the Fquare like the fombereft of Bells: "We have but one Master: Fpirit! No matter how ye may wallow in thine own rigid excremental prifon! No matter how ye may delude thyfelves that Law maketh Morality; Thy lives remain ruled by thine Inner Light. Fin is no other than the darkening of this Light!"\
And now my Lordship, herein lies the remarkable part: Fcarfe had he fpoken these words but that he removed with a great florifh, the blanket from off the Child. Chaos and pandemonium broke out inftantiy; Beneath the blanket lay not a Child as all expected; but a Form of Light!\
Men, Women, Children fcreamed; Ftumbling upon each other in their endeavour to quit their fight of this unnatural Apparition. The Preacher abandoned his grave manner and roared in derifion. At length fome Perfons hearing this laughter and fufpecting they had been duped, turned to ftudy more carefully the Fource of fuch inexpicable and unearthly Light. To their horror they beheld their initial fear to have been well grounded. A ftrange darkness began to furround it as it grew brighter. Cries of "Lucifer!" went up from the crowd as it fled. Within minutes the Fquare was deferted. Only fcattered fhoes and farm implements remaining.\
With the arrival of night the fcattered inhabitants of Ennifkillin gathered together in the furrounding fields elected for the braveft of their party to enter again the Town and afertain whether it were fafe to return. Whether the Devil had left.\

No trace of the Preacher and his Light were to be found.\
Much difcuffion was made upon the feeming impoffibility of fuch collective imagination as could have reafonably explicated the Event.\
There were feveral appearances towards Belfaft and Dublin.

Circumstances of arreft.\
Doubt surrounding end in Drougheda Prifon.
